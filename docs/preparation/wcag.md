# Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)

The **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)** are an international standard developed by the **World Wide Web Consortium (W3C)**. They provide a set of recommendations for making web content more accessible, especially for people with disabilities.

WCAG is widely used as a reference for designing and developing accessible digital products and is often required for legal compliance in many countries.

---

## The Four Principles (POUR)

WCAG is based on four fundamental principles. Content must be:

- **Perceivable** – Information and user interface components must be presented in ways users can perceive (e.g., text alternatives for images).
- **Operable** – Interface components and navigation must be usable (e.g., keyboard accessibility).
- **Understandable** – Information and operation must be clear and predictable.
- **Robust** – Content must be compatible with different devices and assistive technologies.

## Conformance Levels

WCAG defines three levels of accessibility conformance:

### A (Minimum Level)
- The most basic accessibility requirements
- Addresses critical barriers
- Not sufficient on its own for full accessibility

**Example:**

> *(Level A)*
> *Web pages have titles that describe topic or purpose.*

*Source: [Success Criterion 2.4.2 Page Titled](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#page-titled)*

### AA (Recommended Level)
- The most commonly targeted level for websites and applications
- Balances accessibility and practical implementation
- Often required by laws and regulations

**Example:**

> *(Level AA)*
> *Headings and labels describe topic or purpose.*

*Source: [Success Criterion 2.4.6 Headings and Labels](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#headings-and-labels)*

### AAA (Highest Level)
- The most advanced level of accessibility
- Covers additional improvements for enhanced usability
- Not always possible to achieve for all content

**Example:**

> *(Level AAA)*
> *Section headings are used to organize the content.*

*Source: [Success Criterion 2.4.10 Section Headings](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#section-headings)*

## Why WCAG Matters

Following WCAG ensures that digital products are:

- Accessible to a wider audience
- Easier to use for all users
- Compliant with international accessibility standards
- Inclusive by design
