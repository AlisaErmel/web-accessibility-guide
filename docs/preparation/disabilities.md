# User Groups to Consider

When building accessible web applications, it’s important to remember that users interact with your product in **different ways**.

To create inclusive experiences, you should consider several **key user groups**, each with their own interaction patterns and needs.

## Key User Groups

### 1. Vision 👁️

Some users may have difficulties perceiving visual content on the screen.

This means your application should:
- Provide clear structure and meaningful content  
- Support screen readers and alternative text  
- Ensure sufficient contrast and scalable text  

---

### 2. Hearing 👂

Some users may not be able to access audio content.

To support them, your application should:
- Include captions or transcripts for audio and video  
- Avoid relying on sound as the only way to convey information  

---

### 3. Motor & Mobility 🤲

Some users may have limited ability to interact with input devices like a mouse or touchscreen.

Your application should:
- Be fully usable with a keyboard  
- Provide large, easy-to-click interactive elements  
- Avoid time-sensitive interactions when possible  

---

### 4. Cognitive & Learning 🧩

Some users may experience difficulties with understanding, memory, focus, or managing sensory input. This includes individuals with conditions such as ADHD, anxiety, or depression, who may be **very sensitive to bright colors, flashing content, or complex layouts**.

To improve accessibility, your application should:
- Use clear and simple language  
- Maintain consistent layouts and navigation  
- Avoid unnecessary complexity, distractions, or overly bright/flashing elements  

---

## Key Idea 💡

Considering these user groups helps you design applications that are **flexible**, **clear**, and **usable** for a wide range of users.

Accessibility is about anticipating different needs and ensuring your interface works well in all of these scenarios. 💟
